    Yolah Parallel Endgame Solver

    How the exact solver behind the puzzle editor's “Vérifier” shares its search between the cores: the “young brothers wait” scheme of the parallel chess and Othello solvers, a transposition table shared without a lock, the target goals of the puzzles, and the key moves proved in parallel. The sequential solver it builds on is explained in doc/final_solver.{h,cpp}; this one, line by line in French, in doc/final_ybwc.{h,cpp}.

      1What the solver proves

      Every move fills a free square and frees none (its origin becomes a hole), so a position with k free squares ends in at most k moves: when k is small enough, the game can be searched to its very end, and the result is a proof. The solver computes the remaining value of a position, what the side to move will still gain minus what the opponent will, with perfect play. It depends on the board and the side to move only, not on the scores so far, so the transposition table can share it between positions reached with different scores.

      The search is a fail-soft alpha-beta. For a puzzle, only the sign of the final difference matters (win, draw, loss), which a narrow window around 0 decides far faster than the exact value; “win by N” is checked as “win” with N − 1 more points for the opponent. All of this is the sequential solver of doc/final_solver.h, unchanged. What follows is what makes it parallel.

      2Why the moves cannot simply be shared out

      In an alpha-beta search, the first move of a node narrows the window of the next ones, and often it is enough on its own: the node is cut and the other moves are never searched. A good move order makes this the common case. Searching all the moves at once throws that away: the threads search moves that the sequential search would have skipped. Measured on 12 positions of 24 to 26 free squares: sharing out the root's moves between 8 threads is twice slower than one thread (38.1 s against 18.6 s, 2 061 M nodes against 203 M).

      2.1Young brothers wait

      The rule of the parallel chess and Othello solvers (YBWC): the eldest brother, the first move of a node in the move order, is always searched alone, by the thread that is at the node. Only if it has not cut, the young brothers, the other moves, may be shared: the node becomes a split point, and the idle threads come and take its moves one by one. A node is shared only when it has at least 12 free squares (below, its subtree costs less than organising the sharing) and when some thread is idle.

      Each thread searches its move on its own copy of the position and reports the value under the split point's lock: the best value, alpha raised, and, when a value reaches beta, the cutoff. A value obtained with an older, lower alpha is still correct: below that alpha it is an upper bound that changes nothing, above it is exact.

      3Cutoffs and the chain of split points

      When one move of a split point reaches beta, the others are useless. The split point raises its cutoff flag, and every thread working below that node, however deep, must stop. A thread may work under several split points at once (it helps at one, then splits a node of its own subtree), so each split point knows its parent, and stopped(), called at every node, walks the chain.

      A thread stopped this way returns a meaningless value. The search tests stopped() after every move: such a value is never compared, and the node is never stored in the table.

      4The master, its helpers, and the end of a split point

      The split point lives on the stack of the thread that created it, its master: the node cannot be left while a thread still searches one of its moves (workers > 0). The master takes moves like the others; when none is left, it does not wait idle: it helps at the split points opened below its own, by its helpers, the only ones whose end it waits for.

      Two orders matter. A helper joins under the global lock and increments workers before releasing it: the master cannot leave without it. And the master closes the split point (no more moves handed out) before it waits: work_on also returns when the search above is stopped while moves remain, and a thread joining at that moment would work on a split point about to disappear. A helper's last access is the decrement itself, without the split point's lock: once the count is 0 the master may return.

      5The table shared without a lock

      All the threads read and write the same table at every node; a lock would cost too much. An entry is two 64-bit words written one after the other: the data (bounds, move, a “valid” bit) and a check word, the key XOR the data. A reader accepts an entry only if check XOR data gives back the key. When two threads write the same slot at once, a reader may get the data of one and the check of the other: the test fails and the entry is ignored. A torn entry can never pass for a true bound, so the proofs stay exact (the technique of Hyatt and Mann). The valid bit tells an empty slot (two zeros) from an entry: without it, a position whose key is 0 would read the bounds [0, 0].

      6Target goals and the verdict

      A puzzle can mark target squares; a target is taken by the piece that lands on it, once. Besides the points, two goals use them: take more targets than the opponent (Collect), or be the first to land on one (First: that move wins and ends the game). For the solver only the gain of a move changes. The remaining value still depends on the board alone (the targets are fixed for the solver's life), so the table works as it is. Two bounds are new: when no target is in the free regions next to either side's pieces, nothing more can be gained (exactly 0); with Collect, a side blocked for good leaves the other at most the targets of its regions.

      6.1The key moves in parallel

      When the puzzle is feasible and the player moves first, the editor counts the key moves, the moves after which the goal is still reached: one proof per move, all independent. They are spread over the cores, each thread with its own single-threaded solver and table, taking the next move until none is left. When the puzzle is impossible there is no key move, and none is searched.

ETC (a child the table already proves bad enough for the opponent cuts the node before any search) and the ordering by short searches (each move first gets a small node budget; a move proven to reach beta cuts, the moves proven to fail go last) are techniques of the Othello solver Edax: 5 % faster alone, slower with threads, since the short searches are wasted work too.

      7.2Threads, and the false sharing

      The extra nodes of the parallel search are the moves already started when another one wins: 203 M at one thread, 286 M at 4, 440 M at 8, 676 M at 16. Beyond 8 threads this wasted work grows faster than the cores it uses (16 threads: 7.5 s against 4.9 s at 8, in an earlier run), so the editor stops at 8.

      The first version reached only 1.9 times at 8 threads. A core reads memory by lines of 64 bytes: the threads' node counters, written at every node, were neighbours in memory, and the cutoff flag, read at every node by every thread, shared its line with the split point's lock. Each write evicted the line from all the other cores' caches. Giving each its own line (alignas(64)) brought 3.3.

      8Private regions as exact reserves

      Option regions (Points goal; on in the app). A region of free squares only one side can reach is private for good, and its value, the largest number of moves that side can still make in it, is known exactly by the cluster solver of player/solo_region.{h,cpp} (regions linked by a piece touching several, solved with memory, canonical up to translation and the 8 symmetries; built by retrograde analysis for every region of up to 9 squares with 1 or 2 pieces by test/solo_retro_main.cpp). Two exact shortcuts follow: a position with no contested region left is a leaf (remaining = reserve of the side to move − the opponent's), and in each private cluster of the side to move one move is searched, one that keeps the largest reserve: the other moves lead to equivalent positions or to smaller reserves. A cheap test first (the squares each side can reach: no private region, nothing to do), and a direct-mapped cache of the clusters as they stand.

      9Checks

      • test/final_solver_check.sh: the solver with one thread, against its commented sequential copy and a plain negamax: same values, same moves, same node counts (1 000 positions, no mismatch).
      • test/final_ybwc_check.sh: the commented parallel copy, with one thread against EndgameSolver (same nodes), with 8 threads against a plain negamax (values, signs, and the move reaches the value), its verdict and key moves against the negamax, the three goals; and 1 thread against 8 on bigger positions (1 000 + 50 positions, no mismatch).
      • During the development: 8 threads against 1 on positions of 16 to 22 free squares, the three goals (300 checks), under AddressSanitizer; 30 solves stopped in the middle (as “Arrêter” does), then the same solver reused, under AddressSanitizer. The two bugs found that way: a copied Yolah::MoveList keeps its end pointer into the original list (a split point now copies its moves into a plain array), and a thread could join a split point while its master was leaving it (it is now closed first).
      • An outside review (Gemini) found five more defects, all fixed and checked: with a node budget and several threads, a helper reaching its budget dropped its move without a word and its master could conclude without it (now one shared flag stops all: 300 limited solves at 4 threads, 54 incomplete, 0 wrong); with the target goals the value so far defaulted to the score difference instead of 0 (the moves were right, the values shown by “Solveur exact” were not); the pass rule gave a false bound with the scores of a puzzle (it now turns itself off unless the scores are those of a normal game: 1 433 positions, 0 wrong); EndgameSolverPro crashed without a table and ran its short searches without a budget when probe_nodes was 0. A sixth point, a helper falling asleep just after a split, cost up to 200 µs of delay: the split points are now counted (split_gen) and a helper does not sleep when one appeared.
