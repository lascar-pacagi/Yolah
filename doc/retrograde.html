    Yolah Retrograde Analysis

    Chess endgame tables count the positions with few pieces. In Yolah eight pieces stay to the end and only the free space shrinks: tables of whole positions are out of reach. What is small is the private region, the free squares one side only can still reach. This page solves them exactly, builds their table backwards, layer by layer, on the CPU and on the GPU, and uses them in the exact solver, the minimax and the MCTS. The code, commented line by line in French: doc/final_retro.{h,cpp} and doc/final_retro_gpu.cu.

      1Why regions

      A table of every position with only 4 free squares would hold about 1.4 × 1016 entries (doc/solving.qmd): the holes that bound the free space can be anywhere. But late positions fall apart. A queen move only crosses free squares, and two consecutive squares of a line are neighbours (8-connectivity): a piece only ever moves inside the regions of free squares it touches, and regions never merge, since squares only stop being free. A region touched by the pieces of one side only is private for good: an opponent piece can only move to free squares of its own regions, and a square next to ours would belong to ours.

      2Clusters and reserves

      A piece touching two regions will go into one or the other: such regions are not independent. A cluster is a set of regions joined by the pieces that touch several of them, with those pieces (union-find over the regions). A private cluster is a one-player puzzle; its value is the largest number of moves its side can still make in it. It depends neither on the rest of the board nor on when the moves are made: a side whose private clusters are worth M owns M points and M tempi it can spend whenever it likes, a reserve.

      Two exact consequences, used by the players:

      • a position where nothing is contested is settled: its remaining value is the reserve of the side to move minus the opponent's, without any search (checked on 11 585 all-private positions of real games against the exact endgame solver: 0 mismatch);
      • in a private cluster of the side to move, one move is enough to search: one that keeps the largest reserve. Two such moves lead to equivalent positions (only the reserve counts); the other moves of the cluster shrink it.

      3The value of a cluster

      value(cluster) = max over its moves of 1 + value(what is left), 0 without a move. After a move the cluster may split: the values of the parts add up. The search keeps its results in memory, keyed by the canonical form: queen lines depend neither on where a shape stands nor on the 8 symmetries of the square, so the key is the smallest of the 8 transformed shapes (built from three reflections: ranks, files, diagonal), translated to the corner. And it stops as soon as a sequence fills every square: nothing can do better (82 % of the table's states fill their region). Measured: 0.2 µs per position of a real game for all its clusters, with a direct-mapped cache of the clusters as they stand on the board in front of the canonical form.

      4The table, backwards (CPU)

      Retrograde analysis computes from the end of the game backwards: in chess, from the mates. Here the states are ranked in layers by their number of free squares, and a move fills one square: the value of a state of layer n only needs states of lower layers. Computing layer 1, then 2, …, each layer finds everything it needs already solved. The shapes of layer n are grown from those of layer n − 1 (one more square next to the shape, at each placement near the corner where it fits on the board); the pieces stand on the squares around. The shape counts are those of the literature for 8-connected polyominoes (“polyplets”): 1, 2, 5, 22, 94, 524, 3 031, 18 770, 116 452 (with the board's limits) for 1 to 9 squares.

      5The same table on the GPU

      A GPU runs the same small program (a kernel) on thousands of threads at once, each finding its own element from its block and thread numbers; it has its own memory, and the copies from and to it cost. A recursive search with a shared hash table does not fit; layers do. Three steps:

      1. seeds (CPU): every single-region state, canonical;
      2. descent, layer N to 1: one thread per state plays every move and writes its children, normalised (the free squares no piece can reach and the pieces that cannot move removed: they change nothing) and canonical; sorted and deduplicated on the GPU (thrust), they join the layer of their number of squares. Every layer thus holds what the layers above will need;
      3. ascent, layer 1 to N: one thread per state tries every move, finds the child in its layer by binary search (the layers are sorted), and keeps max(1 + its value).

The first GPU version took 556 s at 9 squares, slower than the CPU: each chunk sorted 108 slots per state, almost all empty, and sent every chunk's children back to the CPU, duplicates between chunks included. Compacting before sorting (remove_if) and merging the next layer's children on the GPU (merge + unique) brought it to 11 s; computing the values themselves was never the problem (0.14 s for 88.8 M states). The next step up, 10 squares with 2 pieces (335 M placements), would need a parallel seed enumeration and care with the card's 10 GB.

      Checks: the GPU's canonical form of every seed is the seed (the GPU and the CPU compute the same forms), no child is ever missing, and 2 000 states of each layer recomputed by the CPU solver give the same values.

      6.2The minimax (dev player)

      The dev player already had both ideas: reserve (one move per sealed pocket, kept: +10 Elo in U5) and split solve (the exact leaf, U4 neutral, −12 with reserve in U5). Its harvests were computed by harvest_exact, a new memo per call. Option solo computes them with the cluster solver of each thread, kept between moves. On the bench (209 positions, depth 11): with reserve, the same search (nodes ×1.000, same moves 209/209), time ×0.96; with split solve, time ×1.39 the old way (why it lost), ×0.98 with solo. Campaign U29 (config/search_study/campaign_U29.txt): u29_solo and u29_solo_split against dev_u9_spsa at 0.2 s and 1 s, 2000 games each.

      6.3The MCTS

      Option regions of the AlphaZero search: a leaf where nothing is contested is a finished game for the search (never expanded), worth the exact final difference with the same score utility as the real ends; the root keeps the usual test, so a move is always chosen. Match: nnue/az_regions_match.sh (the app's AlphaZero network with the option against itself without, 2000 games over 5 GPUs, both with the app's score utility).
