    Yolah NNUE Distillation

    How the network of the minimax dev player is made: a large, slow, accurate network, the value/policy ResNet of the MCTS player, gives its value for every position of a big cache, and the small, fast NNUE learns to reproduce these values. The ResNet improves on its own, by self-play; when it is clearly better, it relabels the cache and the NNUE is distilled again. Every stage is shown with its scripts, its targets and its measured results.

    988 Mpositions in the cache, 5 % for validation
    ~10 k/steacher labels per second on one RTX 3080
    1.86 Mparameters of the student (int8 trunk)
    +32.6Elo of the last distillation at 1 s per move
    Contents

      1The chain

      Two networks, two links, nothing going back from the NNUE to the ResNet:

      1. The ResNet learns by itself. The generations loop (nnue/az_gen_loop.sh, its own document az_gen_loop.html) makes the MCTS player play against itself, 12.5 M positions per generation, and trains the ResNet on them and on a pool of older positions, then measures it against the previous generation. The minimax player's games are not used.
      2. The NNUE learns from the ResNet. The ResNet labels the positions of a cache once (nnue/relabel_teacher.sh); the NNUE is trained on these labels and on the games' results (nnue/nnue_persp_distill.sh), exported to the integer file the C++ player reads, checked bit for bit against PyTorch, and measured in matches against the current dev player. The dev player's search plays no part in its training.

      The step from the first link to the second is made by hand: when a generation of the ResNet wins its matches clearly, it becomes the teacher of a new labelling.

      2The cache and its teacher

      The cache cache_az256 holds about 988 M positions of engine games (data/games), each with the game's result z for the side to move. The positions are stored once, in the layout of preprocess_nnue.py:

      FileTypeContent
      inputs.u8(N, 193) uint8black, white, holes (64 bits each, most significant bit first), the side to move
      values.i8int8z, the game's result for the side to move
      teacher_values.f16, teacher_done.u8float16, uint8the teacher's value of each position; which chunks of 1,048,576 positions are labelled
      space_all.u8(N, 92) uint8the candidate space features of each position (space_features compute-all); the student reads 10 of them

      The last 5 % of the positions are the validation set of every training, so that the numbers of two networks can be compared.

      A new teacher. relabel_teacher.sh makes a new cache directory of hard links to every file of the old one but its teacher_* files (no copy, no preprocessing), runs distill_teacher_labels.py with the new network (one forward of the 256×30 ResNet per position, about 10,000 positions per second on an RTX 3080 in fp16: a day for the whole cache on one GPU, half with two), then compare_teachers.py. The present teacher is the AlphaZero 256 network az_00237568 (59,938 s on one GPU); against the game results its mean squared error is 0.250, against 0.256 for the supervised ResNet before it (better in the middle game, worse in the last ten free squares, where the result alone is now the target).

      sbatch nnue/relabel_teacher.sh                                      # the latest az_*.pt of the run
sbatch --export=ALL,TEACHER=/path/az_<step>.pt nnue/relabel_teacher.sh  # another network
sbatch --gres=gpu:2 nnue/relabel_teacher.sh                          # two GPUs

      3The student

      The student is the perspective network of the dev player (minmax_nnue_dev_player.html, section 8): 192 binary inputs seen from each side, a first layer of 1024 values per view, the 10 space features of group 0x46 (queen distance, who reaches first, influence) read by the second layer, 8 heads by phase, a tanh value for the side to move.

      A first layer per phase. The first layer has 4 sets of weights, one per quarter of the game (0–14, 15–28, 29–42, 43–56 holes). In training each set is factorised: a shared set plus a correction of its own, learnt together, so that every position trains the shared part and the corrections only learn what differs (Stockfish's factoriser). The exporter adds them up; the C++ sees 4 plain sets and refreshes the accumulator from a per-thread cache when a move changes the phase.

      Quantization-aware. The forward pass computes what the C++ computes (int8 weights at scale 64, int16 biases, the shifts), the gradient goes straight through the roundings, and after every step the trunk's weights are clamped to ±127/64; so the exported integer network plays exactly as it was trained. These runs are in float32: bf16 cannot hold the 1/64 grid.

      4The loss

      The student is pulled towards the teacher, and the game's result keeps it from inheriting all of the teacher's systematic errors:

      L = α · (v − teacher)² + (1 − α) · (v − z)², α = 0.8

      A weighted sum of two squared errors has its minimum at the weighted mean of the targets: the student aims at 0.8 · teacher + 0.2 · z. With a teacher at 0.30 in a won game (z = +1), it aims at 0.44.

      The endgame: the result alone (Z_FREE=22, the default since 2026-11-12). At 22 free squares or fewer the games of the cache are played perfectly (the result is exact in 99.9 % of these positions), while the teacher errs on 5 to 14 % of them between 18 and 22 free squares. There α = 0: the student learns the exact result. The validation then also reports mse_truth_quantized: the error against z at 22 free squares or fewer and against the teacher above.

      5Training

      Adam without weight decay, batches of 1024 positions, a cosine decay of the learning rate down to a thirtieth over the epochs; PyTorch DDP over the GPUs of the node and torch.compile. The cache is read by chunks of consecutive positions in a random order, each chunk shuffled; the cache's 193 inputs become the two views on the GPU. After each epoch the validation writes one JSON line to <model>.val.jsonl: the mse against the teacher, against z, of the quantized network, and per phase.

      The starting point is one of three, by variant:

      • the older distilled 193-input network (P7=1): its first layer without the turn row, each head a copy of its later layers; the student starts with the old values and learns the rest;
      • a random start (SCRATCH=1): PyTorch's initialisation, seeded;
      • a trained reference (FROM_REF=1): the dev network, its weights frozen (only the corrections by phase of the first layer learn, YOLAH_FREEZE=1) or everything learning (UNFREEZE=1); epoch 0 of a frozen run is the reference exactly, so the gain measures what the new weights bring.

      Each variant writes to its own directory under MODEL_ROOT; a job stopped by its time limit resumes from its last epoch when it is submitted again. A run of 20 epochs on the 988 M positions takes three to four days on one GPU (--time=5-00:00:00).

      7From the trained network to the player

      1. Export. export_value_nets.py --persp model.pt writes model.quantized.txt: a header (the heads, the views, the sizes, the space columns), then every matrix as integers at the scales of the C++ code, the value heads in float; the factorised sets are merged.
      2. Check. The C++ network must give exactly PyTorch's values: nets_check dump draws 2000 random positions, nets_check space-all their space features, check_persp_net.py the PyTorch values, nets_check check_persp compares (|err| 0) and times value() and update().
      3. Match. A configuration of the dev player with the new weights (config/search_study/*.cfg), a campaign file (candidate, reference, time per move, rounds), and nnue/search_study.sh on the CPU nodes; test/search_study_summary.py gives each pair's Elo with a pentanomial interval.

      8The next distillation

      The next teacher will be a generation of the ResNet loop once it has beaten its start clearly in its matches. Then, in order: relabel the cache with it (relabel_teacher.sh TEACHER=…, a day on one GPU), compare the two teachers (compare_teachers.py), distil from the present dev network (FROM_REF=1, everything learning, the endgame on the result alone), export, check, and play it against dev_final.
