    Yolah Generations Loop Internals

    How the value/policy ResNet of the MCTS player improves generation after generation: the current network plays games against itself with its full MCTS search, the endgame solver gives the exact result near the end, and the network learns from the new games mixed with a large pool of older positions that changes slowly. Every stage is shown with its code and its targets.

    12.5 Mnew positions per generation
    + 37.5 Mpositions drawn from the pool for each training
    200 Mpositions in the pool
    1600 / 200simulations of a full / fast search

      1The loop at a glance

      Each generation G starts from the network azG−1 and ends with azG. In four stages:

      1. selfplay: azG−1 plays games against itself with the MCTS search until 12.5 M positions are recorded. Every move is recorded. Positions with at most 22 free squares get the solver's exact result.
      2. train: azG−1 trains for 10 epochs on these 12.5 M positions plus 37.5 M positions drawn from the pool, then becomes azG.
      3. eval: azG plays against azG−1 and against az0.
      4. next: submits generation G + 1 as soon as azG exists, while the evaluation is still playing.

      Generation 0 is ResNet1 of the distillation loop (section 7), which beat its predecessor by +22 Elo.

      1.1Why this loop replaced the distillation loop

      The distillation loop (nnue/distill_loop.sh, doc/distillation_loop.html) generated its games with the minimax dev player and labelled the positions with its search. A search value of the dev player is computed by the NNUE at its leaves. That label carries what the NNUE knows, and the NNUE knows less than the ResNet. The ResNet trained on it gained +22 Elo in one round, but the NNUE distilled from it lost 32.6 Elo (−16.5 with 20 epochs instead of 2). Labels of positions inside a search tree have a second problem: the game's result z is not the result of a position the game never reached.

      The generations loop keeps only what AlphaZero relies on:

      • the games are played by the network being trained, with its own search;
      • every recorded position is a position of the game, so z is its real result;
      • near the end, the exact result replaces z.

      The price is speed: one MCTS move costs a GPU much more than one minimax move costs a CPU core. The pool compensates. Each training sees the new generation as only a quarter of its rows, so the network moves slowly and does not forget what the 200 M older positions taught it.

      2Self-play

      Each self-play task is one GPU running alphazero_learn selfplay: 256 games at the same time, one thread per game, and the network's evaluations gathered into batches on the GPU. The program is the self-play of the AlphaZero learning loop with four options added for this loop.

      2.1The search of a move

      The MCTS player is the one of doc/alphazero_mcts_player.html (PUCT in KataGo's form, graph search, network cache), with the self-play settings:

      SettingValueEffect
      playout cap fast prob0.75playout cap randomization: 3 moves in 4 use a fast search of 200 simulations, 1 in 4 a full search of 1600
      dirichlet alpha, dirichlet epsilon0.3, 0.25noise on the root's priors, full searches only
      temperature, temperature cutoff1, 20the first 20 plies: the move is drawn in proportion to the visits; then the best move
      forced playouts k2KataGo's forced playouts: every child with a prior gets a minimum of visits
      policy target pruningonthe forced visits are removed from π before it is recorded
      graph search, reuse treeon, ontranspositions share a node; the subtree of the move played is kept

      The full searches give the policy targets: many visits, with noise for exploration. The fast searches play the game quickly, which yields more games and so more results z for the same GPU time.

      2.2Where the diversity comes from

      A self-play game starts from the empty board, without a book or random plies (those are used in the matches only). Two sources of chance make the games differ:

      • for the first 20 plies, each move is drawn in proportion to the visits of the root's children;
      • the Dirichlet noise changes the priors of every full search, so the visits differ too.

      The distillation loop, by contrast, used the book plus 2 random plies, because the dev player is deterministic. az_gen_data.py stats measures the diversity of a generation: for each number of moves played, the number of distinct positions among the games (100 % means that no two games share the position).

      2.3The record

      Every recorded move is a TrainingSample of 336 bytes: the position, the result z, the flags, the search's value and its policy π over at most 75 moves.

      In the AlphaZero learning loop only the full searches were recorded. With --record-fast, the fast searches are recorded too, flagged SAMPLE_FAST. Their π is too noisy to be a policy target, but their position has a real result z, so they become value targets. That multiplies the value targets by four.

      2.4The result and the exact result

      When the game is over, every sample gets z, the game's result for its side to move. Then the solver goes through the positions with at most 22 free squares, from the end of the game backwards. The last positions are the easiest, and the solver's table, kept between them, helps with the next ones. The first position that is not solved within 300 ms stops the pass: the earlier ones, with more free squares, would be harder still. A solved position gets z = its exact result and the flag SAMPLE_EXACT.

      While a game thread solves, it asks for no network evaluation. It declares this (solving), so that the GPU's batcher does not wait for it.

      Every position recorded here was played in the game, so z is already its true result when both sides play the network's moves. The counter exact_flipped counts the positions where the exact result differs from z: in a local test of 64-simulation games it stayed at 0. The solver is then mostly a check, and a correction for the rare endgame mistakes of the network.

      Local test (64 simulations per search)Value
      rows per game55
      fast rows75 %
      exact rows38 %
      z changed by the solver0

      2.5The share of a task and resuming

      The 12.5 M rows are split between the tasks: 3 125 000 each with 4 GPUs. A task first counts the rows its files already hold (their names carry its tag, g001t02 for generation 1, task 2), then plays only what is left, with --max-samples. When the count is reached, the game that reached it is still written, and the unfinished games are dropped: every file holds whole games only.

      A task stopped before its share (the 7-day time limit, a node failure) fails. Running bash az_gen_loop.sh G again on the login node submits the generation again, and every task continues from the rows it already has; a task whose share is complete exits at once.

      3The pool

      Training only on the newest games would make the network chase its own latest games and forget the rest. The pool gives the training inertia. It holds 200 M positions; each training uses all of the new generation and 37.5 M positions drawn from the rest of the pool. The pool is not a file: it is a rule that az_gen_train.py applies to the files on disk.

      3.1The rule

      • At the start, the pool is 200 M positions of the old cache cache_az256.
      • Each generation's rows enter the pool and push out as many old positions.
      • With 12.5 M rows per generation, the old positions are all gone after 16 generations. After that, the oldest generations leave the pool.

      The old positions leave in a fixed order: the cache is cut into blocks of 4096 consecutive positions, shuffled once with a fixed seed. The pool's old part is always the beginning of that order, so the blocks at its end are the first to leave, and the old positions of generation G are a subset of those of generation G − 1.

      3.2The draw

      The 37.5 M other rows are drawn from the rest of the pool by whole units: a self-play file (about 2000 rows) or an old block (4096 positions). The units are shuffled with a seed given by the generation, then taken until 37.5 M. Each source therefore contributes in proportion to its size in the pool. Drawing whole units lets the old cache be read sequentially, block after block, rather than one random position at a time.

      The pool and the training of generation G

      The widget assumes exactly 12.5 M rows per generation and gives the expected composition of the draw. The real draw is random, by units, and a generation holds slightly more than 12.5 M rows, because the last games of a task are finished.

      3.3The old positions

      cache_az256 is the old distillation cache: positions of the engine games of data/games, in the layout of preprocess_nnue.py (inputs.u8: 193 bytes per position; values.i8: the game's result z). OldCache.rows turns them into rows of the self-play format with no policy (nb_moves = 0) and the flag OLD = 4, which exists only in Python. Their value target is z, the real result of their game.

      4Training

      az_gen_train.py loads azG−1, reads the training rows into memory (50 M rows of 336 bytes: about 17 GB) and trains for 10 epochs.

      4.1The targets

      RowsValue target (MSE)Policy target (cross-entropy)
      full searcheszπ of the search (weight 1)
      fast searches (FAST)znone (weight FAST_POLICY_WEIGHT = 0)
      solved positions (EXACT)the exact result (z holds it)as their search: π if full, none if fast
      old positions (OLD)z of their gamenone

      With Q_WEIGHT = q > 0, the value target of the non-exact rows becomes (1 − q) · z + q · root_q, the search's own value. It is 0 by default: the value learns the results only.

      L = (v − target)² + Σ w · CE(π, p) / Σ w w = 1 full search, 0 fast search or old position

      4.2The optimisation

      Each row of a batch gets one of the 8 symmetries of the board, drawn at random (BatchMaker). The optimiser is AdamW (weight decay 10⁻⁴, batch 1024), with gradients clipped at 5 and bf16 on the GPU. The learning rate warms up linearly over 500 steps, then follows a cosine from 5·10⁻⁵ down to a tenth of it over the whole training. The exported network is the exponential average of the weights (decay 0.999).

      lr(step) = LR · min(1, (step + 1) / 500) · (0.55 + 0.45 · cos(π · step / total))

      4.3Validation and progress

      One self-play file in 50 of the new generation is kept out of the training (at most 1 M rows). Before the first step and after each epoch, the averaged network is measured on it, and one line goes to ROOT/train_log.jsonl:

      FieldMeasure
      val_mse_zMSE of the value against z, on every row
      val_policy_cepolicy cross-entropy, on the full searches' rows
      val_top1how often the network's first move is the search's most visited move
      val_mse_exactMSE of the value on the solved rows: the error against the truth

      The validation rows are games of azG−1, which the network has not seen. The line at epoch 0 is therefore the old network on the new games, a reference for the following lines.

      4.4Checkpoints

      A checkpoint (gen_G/train_ckpt.pt: weights, optimiser, averaged weights, step) is written every 30 minutes and when SLURM sends its warning signal, 15 minutes before the time limit. The script then exits with code 3. Run again (bash az_gen_loop.sh G train), it draws the same rows (same seeds) and resumes at the saved step. At the end it exports models/az_G.pt and its TorchScript az_G.ts, the file the MCTS player loads, and deletes the checkpoint.

      5Measuring the progress

      After the training, two matches of the MCTS player with alphazero_match.sh, each on a GPU: azG against azG−1 (task 0) and azG against az0 (task 1, from generation 2 on). Each match is 400 games at 1 s per move, from the balanced openings of config/openings/balanced.txt, each opening played with both colours. The results go to ROOT/matches.csv, one line per match with the score and the Elo, and the games to ROOT/evals/. The match against the previous generation says whether the last generation still helped; the match against generation 0 adds up the generations.

      6On the cluster

      az_gen_loop.sh G, run on the login node, submits generation G as a chain of SLURM jobs. Each job runs the same script with its stage, inside the AlphaZero container. The C++ is compiled on the node.

      StageResourcesWaits forWrites

      train1 GPU, 8 CPUs, 96 GB, 4 daysevery selfplay task (afterok)models/az_G.{pt,ts}, train_log.jsonl
      evalGPU array of 2 tasks (1 for G = 1), 8 CPUs, 16 GB, 16 htrainmatches.csv, evals/

      A selfplay task that fails keeps the training waiting forever: SLURM shows the dependency as never satisfied. The fix is to submit the generation again (2.5). Everything lives in ROOT (~/AzGen), and jobs.txt lists the job numbers of every generation.

      7The distillation loop and the NNUE

      The previous loop, nnue/distill_loop.sh (doc/distillation_loop.html), improved both players together: the dev player's games trained the ResNet, and the ResNet then became the NNUE's teacher. It was stopped after round 0.

      ResNet1 is the starting point of the generations loop. The NNUE is not part of the generations loop: the dev player keeps its current network (dev_final).
